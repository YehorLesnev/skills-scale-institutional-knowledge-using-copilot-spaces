# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management Docs hub. If you are onboarding, use this README as your starting point: begin with the overview and then move through the phase guides in lifecycle order. Existing team members can also use this hub as an ongoing reference for planning, execution, communication, quality, and continuous improvement practices.

OctoAcme’s project management framework is designed to ensure repeatable, high-quality delivery across all cross-functional initiatives. The approach is grounded in clear ownership, iterative development, and well-defined success metrics. Each project kicks off with a structured initiation phase, where business needs, objectives, and stakeholders are identified using templates like the Project One-pager. The planning stage then translates ideas into actionable roadmaps, breaking work into shippable increments while identifying dependencies, risks, and responsibilities. Throughout each phase, key artifacts such as the roadmap, risk register, and backlog are maintained to foster alignment and transparency.

The program defines explicit roles and personas to streamline collaboration and accountability. Project Managers (PMs) coordinate delivery, manage risks, and facilitate communications, while Product Managers (PdMs) focus on defining outcomes and prioritizing the backlog for maximum value. Developers are responsible for building and testing features to meet acceptance criteria, and QA/test specialists validate quality through both automated and manual methods. Stakeholders are involved at various checkpoints to supply input, approve decisions, and provide feedback. Responsibilities for each role are detailed, guiding team members on expectations for participation, decision-making, and communication.

Communication at OctoAcme is both regular and structured. Daily standups and weekly delivery syncs keep the team focused on progress, blockers, and dependencies, while monthly updates and ad-hoc escalations ensure stakeholders always have visibility into project status. Escalation paths for risks and blockers are clearly delineated, advancing from team-level triage to sponsor involvement based on impact, with templates provided for incident communications and weekly status reports. These routines promote psychological safety and empower teams to surface challenges early.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout the lifecycle, prioritizing tests—from unit and integration to end-to-end smoke checks—for new features or releases. Automated CI pipelines enforce linting, testing, and security scanning, while manual QA steps are undertaken when required for final acceptance. Each milestone, sprint, and release is accompanied by acceptance criteria and a definition of done, ensuring clarity on what constitutes completion. Post-release, retrospectives and continuous improvement cycles convert learnings into actionable changes, further cultivating a culture of accountability and excellence.
